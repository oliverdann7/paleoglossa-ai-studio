## 02 · Word of the day: μῆνις — `p02-menis.png`

> **μῆνιν ἄειδε θεά** — "Sing, goddess, of the wrath…"
>
> European literature opens on an accusative. Not Achilles, not the goddess — the rage. Homer
> fronts the object because the object is the subject of the poem.
>
> μῆνις isn't ordinary anger (that's χόλος, hot and quick). μῆνις is the cold, sanctioned,
> long-memoried wrath that gods carry — and Homer hands it to a man.
>
> Tap the word in Paleoglossa and you get exactly what's on this card: lemma, full parsing, gloss.
> The morphology comes from the Ancient Greek Dependency Treebank, not a guess.

## 03 · One verse, four scripts — `p03-logos.png`

> ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος.
>
> The same eight words, four ways of writing them — and four different things the sentence can
> be made to mean.
>
> Greek λόγος is word, account, reason, ratio, argument. Latin *Verbum* narrows it to *word*.
> Syriac ܡܠܬܐ (meltha) is word-as-utterance, and it's feminine. Bohairic Coptic — a language that
> borrows Greek vocabulary constantly — declines to borrow here and reaches for its own native
> ⲥⲁϫⲓ (saji) instead.
>
> Translation is the place the meaning leaks out. Reading four versions side by side is how you
> see where.
>
> All four are in Paleoglossa, tokenised word by word.
>
> → paleoglossa.com

`#koinegreek #syriac #coptic #vulgate #peshitta #newtestamentgreek #biblicalstudies #philology
#translation #ancientlanguages`

## 06 · Spaced repetition — `p06-srs.png`

> Reviews timed to the day you would have forgotten.
>
> Most flashcard apps run a fixed ladder — 1 day, 3 days, a week, whatever the app decided in
> 1987. Paleoglossa schedules with FSRS 5, which models the actual decay of *your* memory of
> *that* word and puts the review at the point where recall is about to fail.
>
> Practically: fewer reviews, longer gaps, better retention. And the queue is built from the
> words you met while reading, so it's never someone else's word list.
>
> → paleoglossa.com

## Notes on the claims made above

Everything asserted in these captions is checked against the codebase:

- **12 languages** — `src/lib/data/languages.ts` registers grc, grc-koine, hbo, lat, syr, cop,
  arc, akk, san, egy, hit, uga.
- **90+ complete works** — `public/corpus-data/index.json` lists 93 served texts, plus the
  curated samples bundled in `src/data/corpus/`.
- **Free plan opens every language** — `src/lib/constants/plans.ts`: all plans grant access to
  every language; the free tier caps *tracked vocabulary* at 25 words per language, it does not
  lock texts.
- **FSRS 5** — `src/lib/srs/fsrs.ts`.
- **Knowledge states** — `isTrackedWordState` in `src/lib/constants/plans.ts` (SEEN, LEARNING,
  FAMILIAR, KNOWN) plus NEW.
- **Pronunciation modes** — the four named are the labels shipped in `src/pages/AudioLab.tsx`.
- **Iliad morphology** — `Μῆνιν` really is tagged noun/fem/sg/acc with lemma μῆνις and
  `treebankSource: "AGDT"` in `public/corpus-data/grc-homer-iliad-full.json`.

Don't claim an App Store or Play Store listing in captions — the mobile builds are on
TestFlight / internal testing, not publicly released.
